### 1. The single-instance lock does not survive the database restart the sink now holds through
`market-data-service/src/main/java/dev/amfshr/tradebench/marketdata/store/SingleInstanceLock.java:42` — CONFIRMED

`SingleInstanceLock.acquire` takes `pg_try_advisory_lock` once on `Database.dedicatedConnection()` (a raw DriverManager session) and keeps the connection as its only field; nothing in `main` touches it again until `close()` in the shutdown hook (Main.java:144). A Postgres restart terminates every backend and with it the session-scoped lock; the client learns nothing because no read ever happens on an idle connection (keep-alive and `socketTimeout` only matter during a read). Pre-belt this could not matter: the DB failure killed the pump, Main exited within 60s and the restart re-ran `acquire` (ee35f9b Main.java:140-170). D28 (2) now rules classes 08 and 57 retryable with no time budget — a `docker stop`/restart surfaces as 57P01 then 08006, exactly the hold-through case chapter 10 drills (the 152s stop, "sink recovered after 152s") — and `PostgresStore.recover()` swaps only the store's pooled connection.

Scenario: db sink, Postgres restarted once during a capture day. Capture resumes and the process streams for the rest of its 24/7 life holding no lock. A second `acquire` — another terminal, a stray unit, the restart race playbook §7 records — returns true and a second instance opens a second Lightstreamer connection on the shared key, the exact fail-open the lock exists to prevent (P9). D28, chapter 10 "When the database fails", chapter 05 and chapter 06 never mention the lock across a held outage; `SingleInstanceLockTest` covers cross-session refusal and release only. High because it is a silent fail-open of a P9 guard for the remaining life of the process; medium if a second launch is ruled outside the T7 deploy contract (compose restarts only on exit and would not overlap).

Fix: re-verify the lock on each heartbeat (`isValid` and re-run `pg_try_advisory_lock` on the lock session; exit 1 loud if the session is gone), or re-acquire on a fresh dedicated session after `PostgresStore.recover()` succeeds and exit if refused; a `SingleInstanceLockTest` that restarts the container; a sentence in D28 and chapter 10 on the lock's fate.

### 2. The non-streaming middle states are detector-blind before the first rebuild
`market-data-service/src/main/java/dev/amfshr/tradebench/marketdata/supervise/StuckSubstateEscalator.java:36` — PLAUSIBLE

`StuckSubstateEscalator.onStatus` nulls its substate on any status that is not one of the two retry substates (35-37) and re-enters `WILL-RETRY` with a fresh `enteredNanos` (31-34); the #5 fix stands the watchdog down whenever the connection is not streaming (`Supervisor.applyStatus:381-382` → `StalenessWatchdog.onConnection(false)` → `evaluate` returns nothing at 180-182); `isTerminal` matches only a bare `DISCONNECTED` (ReconnectClassifier:68); and the budget (Supervisor:224) is inert while `consecutiveFailures == 0`, i.e. for any outage that begins from health before a rebuild has run. So every status between "streaming" and `DISCONNECTED:*` — `CONNECTING`, `CONNECTED:STREAM-SENSING`, `STALLED` — is watched by nothing, whether held or cycled.

Scenario A (cycle): the streaming endpoint refuses TCP or the session's `lightstreamerEndpoint` has gone stale. The SDK's retry path emits `DISCONNECTED:WILL-RETRY` → `CONNECTING` (the attempt, failing in ms–4s) → `WILL-RETRY` … each `CONNECTING` resets the 120s stopwatch; the watchdog is down; nothing latches a death; no rebuild ever runs so the budget never starts. Heartbeat reads RECONNECTING indefinitely and `capture_status` stays fresh, so the chapter-10 box-down alarm does not fire either; only a fresh login (a rebuild) could learn a new endpoint. Scenario B (boot hang): `IgStreamControl.start()` returns as soon as `client.connect` is issued (the transport is asynchronous and does not throw for a hanging endpoint); a connection that sits in `CONNECTING` or `STREAM-SENSING` is never rebuilt and never declared dead — chapter 10 row 1 and chapter 08:87 assign the handshake to "the escalator's domain", but the escalator covers only `DISCONNECTED:*`. After any rebuild the same shapes are bounded by the 10-minute budget, so the exposure is the first episode from health or boot. Pre-#5 the watchdog's tick-silence was the net for both. PLAUSIBLE: the code-side mechanism is confirmed line by line; whether the SDK emits `CONNECTING` between retries (its documented lifecycle) or holds `WILL-RETRY` (the playbook's 2h56m specimen "sat" there with auto-reconnect given up) is not settled by the repo and the javadoc was unreachable; the `CONNECTION_STATUS` rows the belt now records will settle it at the first real endpoint outage. No scenario alternates the two statuses; the harness feeds one `WILL_RETRY` and hangs (BeltScenariosTest:166, 188, 213, 273, 412); chapter 10's table has no row for `CONNECTING`/`STALLED`.

Fix: measure time-not-streaming, not time-in-substate — clear the escalator's substate only on `isStreaming` (the D27 one-streaming rule) or a bare `DISCONNECTED`, so the clock accrues across `CONNECTING`; and/or start the recovery budget at the first non-streaming status. Exposing test: alternate `WILL_RETRY`/`CONNECTING` every 4s for 300s and assert a rebuild by 120s; a boot into `STREAM-SENSING` held 300s and assert a rebuild. (Found independently by three angles.)

### 8. A paced-out witness `Rebuild` verdict is silently dropped; nothing re-asks

### 9. A bar-side failure with ticks held is counted as a new episode every ~5s
`market-data-service/src/main/java/dev/amfshr/tradebench/marketdata/ingest/Pump.java:229` — CONFIRMED

Ticks batched (`pending` non-empty) and a bar write fails retryably → `hold()` → `recover()` returns `landsHeld = !pending.isEmpty()` = true because the TICK batch lands (PostgresStore.java:132, 144; the bar is never retried inside `recover`) → `recovered(true)` → `landed()` → `episode = null`, SINK_FAILURE written. The trigger needs a cause that fails `bars_1m` while tick inserts succeed: the upsert blocked >30s by another transaction's row lock on the same (user, source, instrument, start_utc) — a manual repair, or T6's heal writing the same minute — surfacing as 08006 via `socketTimeout`, or a statement-scoped class-57 cancel. Fix: record the write kind that opened the episode and keep it provisional until that kind lands (a bar for a bar failure), or carry `attempts`/`since` across an episode closed in the same hold-return cycle.
